Visualizing 2.3 Million U.S. Wildfires

This project distills nearly three decades of wildfire records into an infographic, interactive dashboard, and slide deck that highlight a stark message: most large wildfires are caused by human activity, and burned acres have climbed relentlessly over time.

Visual Design
Burned palette

The infographic uses a charred, dark background with reds, yellows, and a splash of green to evoke burning forests and regrowth. Data & Metrics
Fires & acres

The visuals summarize aggregated fire records across the U.S., focusing on cause and scale. For clarity, the dataset is filtered down to large fires and a subset of ~50,000 incidents, while still representing the overall trend.

  • Breakdown of fires by cause: human, natural, and unknown
  • Acres burned per cause — human-caused fires burn most acreage
  • Line chart of acres burned per year showing upward trend
  • Number of fires per year visualized as a time series

Table Rock Fire
Personal lens

The slide deck pairs national statistics with a personal anecdote about the Table Rock Fire, grounding abstract numbers in lived experience. The same dramatic fire imagery is used on the title and story slides to make the transition from data to narrative feel seamless.

  • Intro slide: “Increased Risk of Forest Fires”
  • Follow-up slide: “Table Rock Fire” with short anecdote
  • Supports the message that behind every data point is a real landscape
Featured wildfire visualization project
Scale · cause · story

From National Trends to a Single Ridge Line

This project combines a dark, burned aesthetic with clean charts to explain how human-caused fires dominate U.S. wildfire statistics while acres burned continue to rise. The dashboard enables viewers to explore causes, years, and fire sizes, and the Table Rock Fire slides connect the national story to a specific, personal fire event.

Key insight
Human-driven
The majority of large fires and burned acreage can be traced back to human activity, underscoring the impact of policy, education, and enforcement on future fire risk.
