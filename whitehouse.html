Who Visits the White House? Exploring Patterns in Access

This notebook uses publicly released White House visitor records to explore who comes to the White House, when they arrive, and how frequently they return. The analysis combines time-series trends, top visitor lists, and network-style summaries to understand patterns of access across administrations, departments, and organizations.

From Raw Logs to Transparency-Friendly Insights

This project starts with a simple question—who actually gets face time at the White House?— and turns raw visitor logs into interpretable patterns. After cleaning and standardizing the data, the notebook visualizes visit volume over time, identifies top visitors and offices, and summarizes how access is distributed across people and organizations. The result is a set of data-driven transparency insights that go beyond anecdotes and provide a structured view of who shows up, how often, and when.

Data scope
Visits & visitors
Records span many thousands of visits, capturing both one-time guests and frequent visitors, along with the offices they meet with inside the White House.
Insight focus
Volume · Access
Time-series trends, rankings, and simple network summaries work together to show how visitor traffic evolves and which offices sit at the center of the access web.
